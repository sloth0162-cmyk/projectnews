China’s Role in Russia: A Partnership That Calls Itself More Than an Alliance

AP
By Alex Paul
October 2025

China and Russia share one of the most important relationships in the world. Both countries have common interests in energy cooperation, military ties, global stability and a geopolitical alignment that challenges the West.

How the relationship grew

The two countries established diplomatic relations with the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991. Both now describe their ties as more than an alliance, and those ties have deepened since the war in Ukraine.

  1. 1991

    The Soviet Union dissolves and China and Russia establish diplomatic relations. The US–China alliance, which had grown up against the Soviets, comes to an end.

  2. 2001

    The close relationship is formalized with the Treaty of Good-Neighborliness and Friendly Cooperation.

  3. 2008

    China tries to put pressure on Russia after its war with Georgia.

  4. After Crimea

    Russia faces sanctions for annexing Crimea, and Chinese banks become wary of working with Russian institutions. Once China itself is hit with US sanctions, Beijing and Moscow draw closer.

  5. 2019

    The US notices that trade between Russia and China has risen sharply.

  6. February 2022

    The two countries sign a “no limits” economic, military and diplomatic partnership to counter Western influence. Closeness deepens through diplomacy and trade.

  7. June 2024

    The Bank of China halts payments to sanctioned Russian banks, unwilling to risk secondary sanctions.

  8. 2024

    China becomes Russia’s most important economic partner, with bilateral trade soaring to $244 billion.

  9. 2025

    Xi Jinping visits Moscow for the Victory Day parade marking the 80th anniversary of the Soviet victory over Nazi Germany, deepening mutual trust further.

A lifeline under sanctions

As Western sanctions choke Russia’s economy over the war in Ukraine, Moscow increasingly counts on Beijing as a lifeline for energy and raw material exports. After the war began, Russia’s imports rose 63%, and Chinese imports helped bolster Moscow’s wartime economy, binding the two nations closer than ever. Western think tanks believe that without China’s help Russia’s economy would be in deep trouble, and the world sees Russia becoming more dependent on China for trade.

Help, but with limits

It may look as if China is helping Russia fully, but it is not. Many Chinese banks work with Russia carefully, and many exports to Russia are not going through at all. Even so, the help is substantial. Without the restrictions that US sanctions place on China, it could have made Russia even closer and more dependent.

Russia and China have proposed a pipeline to carry 50 billion cubic meters of Russian gas from Yamal in northwest Siberia to China via Mongolia, but China does not seem very interested in the project. A few skirmishes also suggest that China does not fully trust Russia, or does not want it to grow too strong, or did not want US sanctions to land on it.

United by a shared rival

Anti-US sentiment, and a desire to challenge the West, is what pulls China and Russia together in the modern world. Chinese state media were reportedly advised not to publish anything unfavorable to Russia or sympathetic to the West.

China and the war in Ukraine

China does not support Ukraine at the UN, yet it says its position on the conflict is objective, impartial, rational and pragmatic. It has also said it will keep pressure on North Korea and try to stop it sending troops, though this reads more like talk than action. Ukraine, for its part, accuses China of supplying gunpowder and artillery.

The loop: the US, China and now India

History offers a parallel. The US and the Soviet Union fought Nazi Germany as allies, then became enemies. The US then aligned with China against the Soviets, but after the Soviet fall that relationship crumbled, and China is now the biggest rival of the US.

So where does India fit? Either the loop breaks because China wins, or it continues with India taking China’s place.

Conclusion

As of 2025, relations between Russia and China are at an all-time high.

The US in the Indian Ocean: From Acting Alone to Acting Together

AP
By Alex Paul
October 2025

The US used to act unilaterally in its Indian Ocean missions. Now it has shifted to a collaborative approach with India, Australia and Japan through the Quad.

From power projection to regional stability

The shift suggests the US is moving away from power projection and toward regional stability. Rather than interfering directly in the Indian Ocean, it now acts more like a regional security partner.

Why India matters

India is seen as the natural partner to counter China’s influence. The two countries’ ideals for Indian Ocean security do not fully match, but the US needs India to counterbalance China.

The numbers behind the shift

China has surpassed the US in the number of battle force ships, with over 370 platforms, and its fleet is expected to grow to 435 ships by 2030.

During the first Trump administration, the US Pacific Command was renamed the US Indo-Pacific Command. A funding corpus of US$113.5 million to encourage private investment in the region was announced at the July 2018 Indo-Pacific Forum.

Editor’s take

India should collaborate militarily with Thailand, Vietnam and Indonesia.
