A Strategic Axis Across Three Continents

The trilateral partnership between France, India, and the United Arab Emirates represents one of the most innovative minilateral frameworks in the Indo-Pacific. Established formally during the UN General Assembly in September 2022, the grouping fuses European defense expertise, Indian maritime reach, and Gulf sovereign capital.

The Maritime Security Imperative

With the Strait of Hormuz, Bab el-Mandeb, and the Malacca Strait controlling nearly 40% of global commercial energy flow, ensuring sea-lane stability without direct superpower confrontation has become imperative. In June 2023, the trilateral conducted its first trilateral naval exercise in the Gulf of Oman, featuring French Dassault Rafale jets alongside Indian and Emirati naval frigates.

Editorial Board Takeaway

Minilateral coalitions like France-India-UAE offer nimble, non-binding alternatives to rigid Cold War-style treaty pacts, allowing countries to safeguard critical trade nodes without triggering bilateral proxy escalations.

From Hegemonic Hub-and-Spoke to Regional Partnership

For decades following the Cold War, Washington operated primarily through single-state defense treaties and expeditionary forward deployment. In the contemporary Indian Ocean Region (IOR), however, geographic realities and rapid fleet expansion across peer competitors have mandated a distributed alliance posture.

Why India Represents the Anchor Partner

India holds natural geographic dominance over the north Indian Ocean, spanning from the Malacca Strait in the east to the Mozambique Channel in the west. Shared maritime domain awareness (MDA) agreements, mutual logistics exchange pacts (LEMOA), and high-frequency Malabar naval drills underscore the institutionalization of this collaborative framework.

Policy Assessment

Sustained maritime stability in the Indian Ocean will ultimately require expanding collaborative intelligence networks with ASEAN littoral states, including Thailand, Vietnam, and Indonesia.
